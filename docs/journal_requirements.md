# Conservation Biology requirements

Verified 2026-09-25 UTC from current official Society for Conservation Biology
and Wiley pages. Live instructions must be rechecked immediately before
submission.

## Scope and category

- The journal prioritizes work whose conservation relevance transcends the
  focal ecosystem, species, or situation.
- A **Contributed Paper** reports original, data-driven theoretical, empirical,
  or synthetic research in an IMRAD structure with replicable methods.
- Current live limit: **7,000 words** from Abstract through Acknowledgments;
  references, legends, and table bodies are excluded.
- This project is positioned as transferable resource allocation under change
  and partial observability, not as a single-zoo case study or advocacy.

## Review and files

- Double-blind review applies to the manuscript, supporting information, and
  file names. The manuscript, supporting information, and linked review data
  must contain no content identifying authors or institutions except
  unavoidable self-citations.
- A separate cover page carries author information.
- Submission is through ScholarOne:
  <https://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/conbio>.
- The live instructions allow free-format initial submission.

## Style-guide requirements retained unless contradicted by live instructions

The linked Conservation Biology style guide is dated February 2019. Its former
6,000-word limit is superseded by the live 7,000-word instruction. Other
nonconflicting specifications used for the authoring packet are:

- abstract no longer than 300 words;
- 5–8 indexing keywords, excluding words already in the title where practical;
- article impact statement no longer than 140 characters;
- Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion remain separate;
- no standalone Conclusion section;
- concise, declarative title without a colon, dash, question, complete-sentence
  claim, colloquialism, or unexplained jargon;
- supporting information holds technical bulk;
- every in-text citation and literature-cited entry must correspond.
- in-text citations use author-year format;
- parenthetical citations are ordered from oldest to newest and separated by
  semicolons;
- journal titles are written in full.

The legacy guide suggests approximately one main supporting element per four
manuscript pages; the live instructions set no hard display-item limit. At the
authors' request (2026-10-07), all former Supporting Information content is
integrated in the main text, with figures and tables numbered by first
citation. The authors must confirm that this display count suits the final
article.

## Artificial-intelligence policy

The fetched Conservation Biology instructions contain no journal-specific AI
section. Wiley's current publishing-ethics policy therefore supplies the
applicable verified baseline:

- AI may be an additional tool, not a substitute for human expertise and
  judgment.
- Human authors remain responsible for all claims, citations, analyses,
  originality, and the final text.
- Authors should document every AI tool, its purpose, whether it influenced key
  arguments or conclusions, and how they personally reviewed and verified its
  output; AI use must be disclosed at submission.
- AI that substantially edits, develops, or translates any part of a
  manuscript must be described transparently in the manuscript. Tools used
  only for spelling, grammar, and general editing are exempt.
- Authors must verify that the provider claims no ownership or training right
  over submitted content, imposes no incompatible reuse restriction, and
  satisfies privacy and confidentiality obligations.
- AI may be used for data visualizations and illustrations subject to Wiley's
  research-integrity restrictions; editor and journal policy remain decisive.
- AI cannot be an author.
- AI cannot initiate original research without human direction and cannot
  replace accountable human research design, expertise, or judgment.

This repository therefore produces reproducible code, verified source
inventories, results, figures, tables, and a factual human-authoring packet.
Scientific interpretation and the final submission text require accountable
human review and authorship.

## Implementation consequences

1. Keep synthetic simulation evidence separate from empirical source evidence.
2. Do not declare a normative overall winner; report trade-offs and
   assumption-dependent reversals.
3. Trace every proposed numerical claim to `results/manuscript_values.csv`.
4. Submit figures as separate files; all analyses are integrated in the main text.
5. Remove repository/author identifiers from review files and metadata.
6. Include an accurate AI-use declaration at submission.
7. Recheck the live pages and ScholarOne instructions immediately before upload.
8. Preserve the official-source snapshot and retrieval manifest under
   `data/raw/20260925T042308Z/journal_requirements/`.
