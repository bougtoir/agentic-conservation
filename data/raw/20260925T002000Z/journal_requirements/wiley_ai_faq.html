Using Artificial Intelligence in research publishing: your questions answered

Over 300 of you responded to our 2024 ExplanAItions study. 64% agreed that future AI applications could significantly benefit the research community. Our editor respondents showed just as much interest in using AI as other participants, such as identifying gaps in current literature or finding potential collaborators.

At the same time, many raised ethical concerns, including concerns about AI inaccuracies (‘hallucinations’), responsible use, and data privacy. These concerns were echoed by over 900 of you in our recent editor survey.

We know you have questions about how AI should be used, both in your own research and in your role as editors handling submissions.

These FAQs are here to help you find clear, up-to-date answers. We will be continually updating this page as we create new guidance and update our policies on the use of AI.

1. Does Wiley have guidelines on AI use?

Yes, please see our current AI guidelines and the AI section of the Research Integrity and Publication Ethics guide for best practice.

The current ‘common questions and answers’ section is aimed at book authors, with a specific version for journal authors coming soon.

However, many of these common questions and answers will apply to journal authors – such as how to ensure sensitive data and manuscript content is protected, and how authors must declare AI use.

2. Are there AI tools available for researchers – editors, reviewers, and authors – to use?

Yes, please see and share our AI tools comparison resource, which compares AI tools currently available to researchers and their effective use cases during the research process and manuscript preparation.

Institutions may also have enterprise or professional versions of AI tools, which have clearer terms of service and may offer more intellectual property and content protection.

There are many different types of AI tools, which we outline here. Forthcoming guidance will give further advice on how to assess the right AI tools for the right use case

3. What current AI tools are in use on Wiley's manuscript systems?

At Wiley, our Research Exchange platform utilizes AI-powered tools. They function across both our Screening and Review sites – from journal scope checks to a reviewer finder service.

These tools are intended to provide additional information and support editors in making more informed decisions when handling manuscripts.

You can review our Research Exchange and AI resource for more information on each check.

4. Does Wiley have tools to detect AI use within a manuscript?

Yes, integrated into Research Exchange Screening there is an AI-generated content detection tool. This tool scans a manuscript, checking for text that may appear to be AI-generated.

However, please take into consideration that more and more authors use AI tools for spelling and grammar checks – in our recent AI survey, the top use case for AI tools was ‘help with translation’. As such, detecting AI use within a manuscript does not necessarily indicate a potential research integrity issue and/or the quality of a manuscript.

If your concerns are related to potential paper mill content, Research Exchange Screening also offers a problematic phrase check and paper mill similarity detection check. The results of all three checks can then be taken into consideration together to help you better understand and inform your initial decision.

You can review our Research Exchange and AI resource for more information on these checks.

5. What are the acceptable uses of AI for authors?

Authors may use generative AI tools to:

  • Improve grammar, spelling, punctuation, tone, and overall readability of their manuscripts.
  • Use as part of their research methodology (e.g., data analysis, image processing, or modeling).
  • Perform literature discovery.

Please see and share our AI tools comparison resource, which compares AI tools currently available to researchers and their use cases.

Authors must ensure their final work reflects their expertise, voice, and originality. Please see our advice on maintaining author voice when using AI.

For literature discovery, authors must ensure the citations are accurate by always checking the primary resource and that any claims made by the AI about the paper are real and correct.

At Wiley, the acceptability of AI-assisted image creation depends on the image’s purpose and the rights to use the image. In general, authors should document how and when AI-generated images were created, keep a record of the AI tool’s terms of service at the time of creation, and confirm that the AI tool’s terms allow reuse of the generated image. Please see our AI-assisted image creation section for more information.

Please also see our AI principles – which outline our four core principles on AI solutions and technology.

6. Where must authors disclose their use of AI, and what do they need to confirm in their declaration statement?

If an author has used AI technology to develop any portion of a manuscript, its use must be described, transparently and in detail, in the Methods section (or via a disclosure or within the Acknowledgements section, as applicable). This includes generating supplementary materials, such as images, tables, or charts, substantial editing of existing content, or performing significant analyses.

The authors should disclose the name of the AI tool used, its purpose, whether it impacted key arguments or conclusions, and how they personally reviewed and verified AI-generated content. Authors must disclose the use of AI tools upon submission to a Wiley journal.

Authors must also confirm that the platform and/or provider of the AI tool does not claim ownership of their content or impose limitations on its use, as this could interfere with the author’s rights or Wiley's rights, including Wiley’s ability to publish a submission following acceptance.

Please note that AI tools that are used to improve spelling, grammar, and general editing are not included in the scope of these disclosure guidelines.

Please see here for more information, as well as an example declaration.

7. Can peer reviewers use AI writing tools?

Neither editors nor peer reviewers should upload manuscripts (or any parts of manuscripts including figures and tables) into AI technology, since it may use input data for training or other purposes. This could then violate the confidentiality of the peer review process, privacy of authors and reviewers, and the copyright of the manuscript under review.

However, an editor or peer reviewer can use AI tools to improve the spelling and grammar, tone, and clarity of their written feedback. A reviewer’s use must be declared upon submission of the peer review report to the manuscript’s handling editor and an editor must declare this upon submitting their written feedback.

The peer review process is a human endeavor, and reviewers are ultimately responsible and remain accountable for providing a peer review report. This process should not be delegated to a GenAI tool.

Please refer to the AI section of our Best Practice Guidelines on Research Integrity and Publishing Ethics for further information.

8. Can the accuracy of AI-produced or AI-edited content be trusted?

AI models can generate content that sounds authoritative but contains factual errors, invented citations, or outdated information. These are sometimes called ‘hallucinations’.

It should also be noted that AI models can reflect biases – linguistically or broad stereotypes and generalizations; this bias can be present in the data the model was trained on and/or from the user’s perceived bias (sometimes called sycophantic behaviour). Authors should check AI responses to identify potential biases and revise their content accordingly.

Authors are ultimately responsible for ensuring the accuracy of their final work.

Please see and share our advice on verifying accuracy of AI-generated content.

9. How can researchers securely protect their information and content from AI tools?

Some AI tools may include clauses in their terms of service that grant the AI provider rights to reuse, distribute, or train their AI technology on content processed through their platform. Please see the privacy features and IP protection sections for more information on understanding common terms of service phrases and how to ensure content is protected.

Authors must use AI tools in a manner that aligns with privacy, confidentiality, and compliance obligations. For researchers working with sensitive content, private details from text or data sets should be removed before using publicly available generative AI tools. Please see our safe use section for more information on safe and responsible use of AI tools.

Institutions may also have enterprise or professional versions of AI tools when available, which have clearer terms of service and may offer more intellectual property and content protection.

10. Do AI tools 'own' the content or the text they generate?

AI technology cannot be considered capable of initiating an original piece of research without direction by humans, nor does it have legal standing or the ability to hold or assign copyright.

However, some providers claim ownership and/or impose limitations on the use of the underlying content provided to and/or generated by their AI tool. This could interfere with the author’s rights or Wiley's rights, including Wiley’s ability to publish a submission following acceptance.

Please see the IP protection section on our AI guidelines for more information.
